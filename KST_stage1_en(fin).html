K-SemiTech Onboarding Learning System

Stage 1 — Adult Learning & Workplace Learning

Industry Background

K-SemiTech (KST) is a semiconductor and precision-equipment manufacturer with a 20-year track record. Over the past three years, the company has been rapidly adopting AI-based process automation, and it is now facing two changes at once. First, hiring criteria have shifted from education and tenure toward job competency and growth potential, bringing in a more diverse mix of new and experienced employees. Second, as automated equipment replaces existing processes, tension is growing within the organization over whether the tacit knowledge veteran engineers have built up over decades on the floor is still a valid asset — or whether it needs to be reconstructed.

The company has long run an onboarding system that combines live-in training with on-site mentoring for new hires. But recent feedback from the HRD team has raised doubts about how well that system is actually working.

Core Dilemma — Lee Seo-yeon, who leads training design on the HRD team, has to redesign next year's new-hire onboarding program. Then an AI-based anomaly detection system is introduced on the line run by Park Hyun-woo, an 18-year veteran process engineer — and the system flags a process anomaly that Park had judged to be fine. It leaves Park with a fundamental disorienting dilemma: "the way I've made judgment calls for almost 30 years might not be enough anymore." Lee now has to look beyond individual differences in how people learn and ask a bigger question: how should the organization redesign its learning system altogether?
👨‍🔧

Park Hyun-woo

Veteran Process Engineer · 18 years
"I was twenty-eight when I first got assigned to this line. My mentor at the time, Manager Choi, taught me to listen to the sound before I ever looked at the gauges. The moment a motor's sound changes even slightly, you can tell something's wrong — even while the numbers are still in the normal range. How do you even write that down in a manual?"
"Last month... the AI system caught an anomaly before I did. In a process I'd judged to be fine. At first I thought it was a glitch. But when we checked, there really was an early-stage anomaly. The machine knew something I didn't."
"I keep thinking about it since then. Was what I trusted for almost 30 years really 'insight born of experience' — or was I just lucky? I hate looking shaken in front of the younger engineers. But honestly... I'm not even sure what I should be teaching Ji-soo right now."
👩‍💻

Kim Ji-soo

New Engineer · 6 months
"I need to understand the principle behind something before I can move forward. Even in college, I always wanted to fully understand the theory before running an experiment. But here, all I hear is 'just try it' or 'watch and learn.'"
"Hyun-woo isn't a bad mentor at all. But every time I ask him something and he says, 'well... it's just something you feel,' I get lost. I'm under pressure not to make mistakes, but without a clear explanation of why, it just makes me more anxious."
"There is online training material, but it's too generic — it doesn't match what's actually happening on the line. I look things up on YouTube and read papers on my own, but I'm never sure if I'm on the right track... and if I don't do even that, I feel like I'm not learning anything at all."
👩‍💼

Lee Seo-yeon

HRD Training Design Lead
"I've scrapped three drafts of the onboarding redesign already. My first idea was just 'do more on-site mentoring' — but once I realized even veterans like Hyun-woo are shaken right now, that alone clearly isn't enough. More online content isn't the answer either — Ji-soo's already looking things up on her own way too much."
"I don't think this is a question of who's right. Both of them are doing their best in their own way. The real problem is that their two approaches aren't connecting with each other."
"Honestly, I'm not sure either. The company wants an 'efficient onboarding program,' but I have a feeling this isn't something one program change can fix. Maybe we need to rethink how the whole organization learns."
